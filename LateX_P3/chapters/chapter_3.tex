\section{Requirements and Specifications}

\textbf{Data.} The system is trained and evaluated on the pre-processed EAV release \cite{Lee2024EAV}: one file per participant and modality, 42 participants, 400 five-second trials per participant in each modality, 80 per emotion class. Audio is stored as raw waveforms resampled to 16 kHz (80{,}000 samples per trial); the AST feature extractor converts each waveform to a 128-band log-mel representation when the data are loaded. Video is stored as 25 frames per trial. EEG is stored as 30-channel windows downsampled from 500 Hz to 100 Hz and band-pass filtered to 0.5--45 Hz. For the within-subject protocol the EAV repository's class-stratified split assigns 280 trials per participant to training and 120 to testing.

\textbf{Compute.} All training ran on Google Colab Pro, mostly on an NVIDIA L4 GPU with 24 GB of memory. Development machines---first an Apple M1 laptop, later a Windows PC with an Intel i7 processor and 32 GB of RAM---had no usable GPU, so code was written locally, versioned on GitHub, and executed remotely, with data, checkpoints and results kept on Google Drive (Section~\ref{sec:path_configuration}).

\textbf{Software.} PyTorch 2.10 and Hugging Face Transformers provide the models; torchaudio, torchvision, librosa, NumPy, pandas, SciPy and scikit-learn support preprocessing and analysis. The EAV repository's loaders and single-modality trainers are used with the corrections listed in Chapter~\ref{chap:engineering_challenges}; the fusion module and every analysis script are separate code written for this thesis.

\textbf{Training budgets.} Within-subject encoders: AST 5 epochs with a frozen backbone then 5 fine-tuning epochs; ViT 3 then 2; EEGNet 350 epochs from scratch. Within-subject fusion heads: 80 epochs of AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, cosine schedule, batch 32), scored once at the final epoch. Cross-subject encoders, retrained for every fold: AST 2 then 2 epochs; ViT 3 then 2, with the best fine-tuning epoch chosen on inner-validation participants; EEGNet up to 150 epochs with early stopping (patience 25). Cross-subject fusion heads: up to 40 epochs at batch 64, best epoch chosen on the inner-validation participants. The full within-subject rollout took about 12 hours of GPU time.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} All code is in the repository \texttt{shafinmuffinn/thesis\_p2}. Every file path is read from environment variables through one module, so the same scripts run locally and on Colab. Per-trial logits, features and trained fusion heads are cached, so every fusion, calibration, audit and statistics result in this report can be regenerated without retraining the encoders.

\section{Constraints}

\textbf{Compute and session limits.} Colab sessions end after a fixed time or when idle, and a single cross-subject fold needs several hours of GPU time. Every stage therefore writes its outputs to persistent storage and resumes where it stopped, and the five folds were spread across separate sessions.

\textbf{Data.} All experiments use one laboratory corpus with cued, performed emotions. A planned acquisition of new EEG recordings was cancelled for budget reasons; low-cost consumer headsets were considered and rejected because their few electrodes do not match EAV's 30-channel montage. Generalisation beyond EAV is therefore untested.

\textbf{Per-participant data volume.} Each participant contributes 400 trials. Within-subject models learn from 280, which limits what a learned fusion module can extract, and cross-subject models see each test participant only through whatever calibration data they provide.

\textbf{Time.} The final phase had a fixed defence deadline, which ruled out experiments needing new hyperparameter searches over encoder training, such as contrastive pre-training or sequence-level cross-modal attention (Section~\ref{sec:fusion_design_rationale}).

\section{Societal and Clinical Impact}
The societal impact of this research runs through three channels.

\textbf{Emotion recognition that works for new users.} Any deployed emotion recognition system meets people it was not trained on. The subject-independent evaluation of Chapter~\ref{chap:crosssubject} measures how much accuracy is lost in that situation on EAV, and the calibration method of Chapter~\ref{chap:calibration} recovers most of it with a short unlabelled enrolment recording and no labels. For applications such as adaptive human-computer interfaces, this is the difference between a system that works on its developers and one that works on its users.

\textbf{More reliable research practice.} The audit of Chapter~\ref{chap:audit} shows how easily a plausible result on this benchmark can be produced by test-set model selection, and how easily a map of one classifier's errors can be read as a map of human emotion regulation. The tests it uses are cheap and reusable, and they apply to every published fusion result on EAV.

\textbf{Mental health monitoring, with caution.} Detecting divergence between expressed and felt emotion remains a clinically motivated goal, relevant to conditions such as alexithymia and depression in which self-report is unreliable. This thesis does not provide such a tool. Its contribution to that goal is negative but useful: it shows that classifier disagreement alone is not evidence of suppression, and specifies the permutation test and the independent internal-state measure that a future clinical study would need before any claim could be made.

\section{Ethical Considerations}
EAV was collected by Lee, Shomanov, Kabidenova, Yazici and colleagues at Nazarbayev University under ethical approval, with participants' informed consent to recording and to the research release of de-identified data. Access is by request to the dataset's authors; for this thesis it was granted by Adai Shomanov through a token-protected Zenodo link, with permission to use the data for this research. Only the released, pre-processed files were used, and no new data were collected from human participants.

Two further considerations follow from the results. First, the features learned for emotion recognition identify the individual: a linear classifier recovers which of the 42 participants produced a trial with 97--100\% accuracy from audio or video features and 82\% from EEG features (Section~\ref{sec:cal_mechanism}). Stored emotion features are therefore personal data, and any deployment must handle them as such; the calibration method additionally requires an enrolment recording of each user, which needs informed consent and secure handling. Second, coherence or suppression analyses applied to clinical populations raise considerations of privacy, stigmatisation, and clinical deployment without appropriate validation---all the more so given that Chapter~\ref{chap:audit} finds the present suppression matrix to be indistinguishable from classifier error. These are explicitly out of scope for the present thesis, which is confined to analysis of laboratory data. Any future work involving clinical populations must obtain independent ethics approval and must not make clinical inferences based solely on the present thesis's findings, which have not been validated against clinical ground truth.
